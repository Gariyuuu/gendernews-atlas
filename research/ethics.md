# Ethics statement

## What is measured, and what is not

The project measures **how newspapers signalled gender** through honorifics, pronouns
and gendered nouns, as a property of the *text*. It never infers anyone's gender
identity. It does not use first names, photographs or external records to assign
gender. People the text does not gender are kept as UNKNOWN and reported.

The honorific system of 1900–1963 American English was binary and tied to marital
status for women (Mrs./Miss). The binary categories in our results reflect that
convention. They are not a claim that gender is binary, and the text gives no way to
recover the identities of people it misgendered or erased.

## Data and rights

- **Source text**: pre-1964 U.S. newspapers digitised by the Library of Congress
  (Chronicling America), which are in the public domain in the United States.
- **Dataset**: AmericanStories (Dell et al. 2023), licensed CC-BY-4.0. We attribute it
  in the README, on the site's data page, and in the paper.
- **Redistribution**: the public repository contains article IDs, metadata, derived
  features, aggregate results and short context excerpts. The raw sampled text is
  regenerated by `make data` rather than committed, for size, not rights.
- **The subjects are real people**, nearly all deceased. The site shows short
  excerpts only as evidence for model labels. It supports no search by personal name
  and no person-level profiles.

## Historical content

Historical newspapers contain racist, sexist and violent material, including coverage
of lynching and demeaning descriptions of women and minorities. Excerpts appear
unedited because they are evidence; the site warns readers of this on the evidence
pages. The women most visible in this corpus (society pages, club news) were
disproportionately white and affluent. Aggregate "women's representation" figures do
not describe all women.

## Use of language models

- The optional LLM condition uses the **open-weights Qwen3-8B** model. Requests go through
  the owner's gateway (api.gariyuuu.com), which forwards them to a **hosted inference
  provider (OpenRouter)**. Corpus excerpts therefore do reach a third-party service. They
  are public-domain newspaper text, so no rights or privacy restriction applies, but
  the routing is disclosed so no one mistakes it for local inference.
- LLM outputs are one measurement condition among five, validated against reference
  labels, and never treated as ground truth.
- The build agent that wrote this code also produced the v1 reference labels. That
  conflict of interest is disclosed in `annotation_protocol.md`; the mitigations are
  blinding and a planned human re-annotation.

## Interpretive restraint

Changes over time are described, not explained. Historical events such as suffrage
(1920) and the wartime labour shifts of 1942–45 are context only and are never
presented as causes. Claims that do not survive the robustness grid are reported as
negative or tentative results, not dropped.
